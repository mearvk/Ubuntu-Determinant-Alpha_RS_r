## Settlement Class

The archive uses **settlement class** as a descriptive social/economic classification field, not as a legal determination about an individual or group. For this 1966 record, the applicable classification vocabulary may include **Democrat, Millionaire, Professional, College/Academic, Government/Public Institution, Worker, Farmer, Business/Commercial, Legal, Congressional/Public Policy, Institutional, or Other** when a classification is relevant to the subject being documented. A class designation must not be inferred merely from wealth, occupation, political affiliation, or reputation; where no reliable classification applies, use `UNCLASSIFIED`.

For historical documents, political labels such as `Democrat` should be treated as a classification of the referenced person, institution, or source when independently established—not as a characterization of the statistical data itself. `Millionaire` likewise denotes a wealth/status classification only when supported by an appropriate historical source. These labels are metadata, not conclusions about moral worth, legal status, or scientific validity.

## Science Grade

**Science Grade: 6+**

The `6+` grade indicates that the archive is intended to meet a substantial evidence-and-methodology standard: identify the source, preserve units and definitions, distinguish observation from reconstruction, disclose uncertainty, and avoid manufacturing unavailable historical data. It is a documentation-quality grade rather than a claim that every underlying historical statistic is experimentally measured or universally accepted. Statistical, historical, economic, legal, and social evidence may therefore coexist, provided their evidentiary status is clearly distinguished.

## Methods Intent — WHY

The reason for maintaining this archive is to make historical information usable across time and across disciplines without losing the context that gives a number meaning. It is designed for **college and professional study**, where a researcher should be able to trace an observation back to its statistical or documentary basis; for **long-term legal and historical research**, where provenance, definitions, dates, and revisions may matter decades after publication; and for **Congressional, governmental, and institutional inquiry**, where a durable record can support comparison, oversight, policy analysis, and independent review. The objective is not simply to accumulate numbers, but to preserve an auditable record of what was reported, by whom, under what definition, and with what limitations.

The broader reason is moral as well as technical: historical records should not be made more convenient by quietly changing what they said. A future reader should be able to distinguish an original observation from a later reconstruction, an official statistic from a secondary estimate, and documented fact from interpretation. This archive therefore treats provenance and uncertainty as part of the data itself. Its purpose is to provide a stable foundation for learning, professional judgment, lawful inquiry, public accountability, and responsible historical understanding.

## Document Reference Rule

**All documents in `china/1966/` should refer to `META.md` for shared metadata and classification.** Individual category files may add subject-specific metadata, sources, definitions, and methodological notes, but should not contradict the controlling fields in this file. When a category requires a different source-specific definition, the category document must identify that difference explicitly.

## Source Classes

- **Primary:** official statistical agencies, contemporaneous official publications, original reports, and archival records.
- **Secondary:** scholarly books, academic papers, institutional research, and historical compilations that interpret or reproduce primary material.
- **Reconstructed:** later statistical series that estimate, harmonize, index, or otherwise reconstruct historical observations using a stated methodology.
- **Reference:** methodological or contextual material used to understand the archive but not necessarily to establish a numerical observation.

## Source Discipline

The archive follows the principle that statistical documentation should retain provenance, scope, definitions, methods, and historical changes alongside observations. No person, organization, political classification, wealth classification, or moral conclusion should be assigned merely because it appears convenient for categorization. Classifications must be source-supported and kept separate from the statistical validity of the underlying data.
