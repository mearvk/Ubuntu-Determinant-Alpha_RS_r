## Settlement Class
Settlement class is a descriptive social/economic classification, not a legal determination. Vocabulary may include **Democrat, Millionaire, Professional, College/Academic, Government/Public Institution, Worker, Farmer, Business/Commercial, Legal, Congressional/Public Policy, Institutional, or Other** when source-supported. Do not infer class from wealth, occupation, political affiliation, or reputation; use `UNCLASSIFIED` when unsupported. Political and wealth labels apply only to independently established referenced persons, institutions, or sources, not to statistical data, and are not moral or legal conclusions.

## Science Grade
**Science Grade: 6+** — a documentation-quality grade requiring source identification, preserved units and definitions, distinction between observation and reconstruction, uncertainty disclosure, and no manufactured historical data. It does not claim that every underlying statistic is experimental or universally accepted.

## Methods Intent — WHY
The archive exists to make historical information usable across time and disciplines without losing the context that gives a number meaning. It is designed for **college and professional study**, **long-term legal and historical research**, and **Congressional, governmental, and institutional inquiry**, so observations can be traced to their documentary basis and used for comparison, oversight, policy analysis, and independent review.

The broader reason is moral as well as technical: historical records should not be made more convenient by quietly changing what they said. Readers should be able to distinguish original observation from reconstruction, official statistic from secondary estimate, and documented fact from interpretation. Provenance and uncertainty are therefore treated as part of the data.

## Document Reference Rule
**All documents in `china/1973/` should refer to `META.md` for shared metadata and classification.** Category files may add subject-specific sources and methods but should not contradict controlling fields here.

## Source Classes
- **Primary:** official agencies, contemporaneous official publications, original reports, archival records.
- **Secondary:** scholarly books, papers, institutional research, historical compilations.
- **Reconstructed:** later series that estimate, harmonize, index, or reconstruct historical observations using stated methods.
- **Reference:** methodological or contextual material.

## Source Discipline
Retain provenance, scope, definitions, methods, and historical changes alongside observations. No person, organization, political classification, wealth classification, or moral conclusion should be assigned merely for convenience; classifications must be source-supported.
