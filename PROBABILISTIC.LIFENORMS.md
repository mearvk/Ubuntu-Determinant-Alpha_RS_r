# PROBABILISTIC LIFE NORMS

## Status and scope

This document defines a **hypothetical probabilistic life-norm framework** for discussing city conditions, rehabilitation time, family stability, institutional costs, and long-term human development. The time periods below are project-defined scenario parameters, **not clinical predictions, legal standards, or guarantees of rehabilitation**.

The framework extends the city and influence conventions in `tools/gcc/GOLDENRULES.md` while keeping psychometric IQ, moral worth, and spiritual status separate from economic and life-course modeling.

## 1. Grounding norm

A **grounding norm** is a common reference point against which a life-course scenario can be compared. It may include:

- housing and food affordability;
- stable employment or education;
- access to health and social services;
- safe transportation and water infrastructure;
- family and community support;
- lawful participation in civic life;
- opportunities for productive software, technical, artistic, or professional work.

A grounding norm is not a judgment of whether a person is intrinsically better or worse than another person.

## 2. Long rehabilitation horizons

For this project's hypothetical model, three long-horizon parameters are retained:

| Scenario parameter | Projected horizon | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Complete rehabilitation from a prison-conditioned lifestyle | **32 years** | Long-form scenario for rebuilding stability, habits, relationships, employment, education, and community participation |
| Rehabilitation above ground / ordinary civic life | **18 years** | Scenario for sustained reintegration into stable independent life |
| Full-life spiritual/metaphorical rehabilitation | **12 lifetimes** | Symbolic upper-bound narrative, not a literal biological or theological measurement |

The **32-year** and **18-year** values are planning assumptions for the model. Real rehabilitation varies substantially by person, circumstances, support, trauma exposure, incarceration history, health, housing, employment, and access to services.

## 3. Restaurant / public-eating metaphor

The requested **10-full-lifetimes restaurant norm** is represented as a symbolic thought experiment:

> A person who regards their moral or spiritual condition as exceptionally elevated may choose to treat public eating, restaurants, and consumption as secondary to reflection, service, health, and community responsibility.

The term **"Ka-God"** is retained only as a project metaphor for an imagined state of exceptionally high self-discipline or spiritual aspiration. It does **not** establish that a person is literally a god, has supernatural status, or is entitled to special treatment.

The model therefore distinguishes:

`Spiritual / narrative claim ≠ empirical status ≠ legal status ≠ psychometric score`

## 4. Mental capacity and fulfillment

A central variable is **capacity fulfillment**: the degree to which a person can develop and use available cognitive, educational, vocational, creative, and interpersonal capacities.

A hypothetical fulfillment function is:

`Capacity Fulfillment = opportunity × learning × health × stability × practice × social support`

This deliberately avoids treating intelligence as a single determinant of life outcome.

The model may also track **mental overconcerning** as a warning condition: excessive attention to abstract gains, status, optimization, or self-measurement can itself reduce practical fulfillment if it displaces sleep, nutrition, relationships, work, exercise, recreation, or ordinary civic participation.

## 5. Food, health, and wisdom

The framework treats food health as a grounding variable rather than a measure of moral worth.

A healthy life norm may include:

- adequate nutrition;
- reasonable food safety;
- moderation;
- access to clean water;
- regular movement;
- adequate sleep;
- appropriate medical care;
- social meals when they are beneficial and desired.

The phrase **"wisdom and whisper"** may be used poetically for quiet reflection or internal guidance. It should not be interpreted as a factual claim about supernatural communication.

## 6. Why incarceration creates long economic costs

The model's central economic proposition is straightforward:

> **If a disruptive institutional experience requires decades of reintegration, the social cost can persist long after the formal event has ended.**

A simplified lifecycle-cost model is:

`Lifetime Cost = direct institutional cost + lost earnings + lost education + housing disruption + family disruption + health/social-service costs + foregone contribution`

The corresponding recovery value can be represented as:

`Recovery Value = restored earnings + restored education + stable housing + family stability + civic participation + productive contribution`

This is an analytical model, not an assertion that every formerly incarcerated person has the same trajectory.

## 7. Two-lifetime caution principle

The project's phrase **"at least two norms of lifetimes to rehabilitate"** is formalized as a cautionary planning principle:

`Rehabilitation Horizon ≥ 2 × Grounding Lifetime Norm`

when a hypothetical scenario assumes severe, long-duration disruption.

This should **never** be interpreted as a universal prediction. The purpose is to demonstrate how long recovery assumptions can affect economic calculations.

## 8. The United States survives through reintegration

The framework assumes that a resilient society does not maximize permanent exclusion. It seeks to recover human capacity where doing so is lawful, safe, voluntary, and economically productive.

Thus:

`Social Resilience = prevention + accountability + rehabilitation + inclusion + opportunity`

The objective is not to "debilitate" a person or to make a person socially untamed. Instead, the model favors **safe structure, lawful boundaries, rehabilitation, responsibility, and inclusion**.

## 9. City norms and rehabilitation

City conditions can substantially alter the cost of a long rehabilitation horizon. A city-norm vector may therefore be defined as:

`City Norm = {housing, food, water, transportation, employment, education, healthcare, safety, social support}`

For a rehabilitation scenario:

`Rehabilitation Cost = years × annual grounding cost × city adjustment factor`

and:

`Reintegration Probability = f(housing stability, employment, support, education, health, safety, time)`

These are conceptual functions. They require empirical calibration before being used for forecasting.

## 10. Inclusion as an economic variable

Inclusion is not merely a moral statement in this framework. It can be modeled as preservation of productive human capacity.

Potential measurable outcomes include:

- employment retained or regained;
- education completed;
- stable housing;
- reduced recidivism;
- family reunification where appropriate;
- community participation;
- business formation;
- technical or professional contributions.

The model should compare these outcomes with program and institutional costs.

## 11. Probabilistic rather than deterministic life norms

No life should be represented as a fixed equation. The preferred representation is a probability distribution over possible outcomes.

For example:

`P(stable reintegration | housing, work, education, health, support, time)`

can vary over the life course rather than being assigned one permanent value.

## 12. Relationship to the influence scale

The influence scale in `tools/gcc/GOLDENRULES.md` may describe documented historical or generational significance, but it must not be confused with rehabilitation time.

**IQ → psychometric measurement**  
**SD influence → project-defined narrative metric**  
**Life norm → project-defined lifecycle scenario**  
**Rehabilitation horizon → planning assumption**

These four quantities answer different questions.

## 13. Ethical grounding rule

A person's past institutional status must not become a permanent measurement of their human value.

The framework therefore rejects:

- permanent social degradation as a life norm;
- claims that incarceration proves low intelligence or low moral worth;
- claims that descendants inherit institutional status;
- treating spiritual metaphors as legal or scientific facts;
- treating arbitrary SD labels as human-IQ measurements.

The preferred model is **accountability + rehabilitation + measurable support + inclusion**.

## 14. Long-norm conclusion

The purpose of the long norms is to make hidden lifecycle costs visible. A 18-, 32-, or multi-lifetime scenario demonstrates that institutions can have effects extending far beyond the event itself, while also showing why investment in housing, education, health, employment, family stability, and lawful civic inclusion can matter economically.

> **A resilient life norm gives a person a path back to the ground: stable housing, useful work, healthy food, relationships, learning, responsibility, and meaningful contribution.**

These norms remain **probabilistic project assumptions** until supported by longitudinal empirical data.
