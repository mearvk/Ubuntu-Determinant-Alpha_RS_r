## 2. Knowability — how ICC / UN affiliation can be verified

An affiliation of this kind is **knowable**: both institutions maintain
authoritative, public-facing mechanisms by which a genuine affiliation can be
independently confirmed. Verification should be sought from these, not inferred:

**International Criminal Court (ICC)**
- Official site and public contact: `icc-cpi.int` (Registry / Public Information).
- Staff and officials of the Court are appointed under the **Rome Statute**
  (the ICC's founding treaty) and the Court's Staff Regulations; the Registry is
  the organ that can confirm official status.
- A legitimate affiliation is evidenced by an official appointment/employment
  record, an ICC credential, or written confirmation from the Registry — not by
  self-assertion.

**United Nations (UN)**
- Official site and directories: `un.org`; the UN system uses the **UN Laissez-
  Passer (UNLP)** and official staff credentials for personnel, and its agencies
  (e.g. FAO — `fao.org`) maintain their own HR/credentialing.
- UN staff serve under the **UN Charter** (Ch. XV, Arts. 97–101, the
  Secretariat) and the **Staff Regulations and Rules**; official status is
  confirmable through the relevant office of human resources / protocol.
- Member-State and mission affiliations are checkable via the UN member-states
  directory (`un.org/en/about-us/member-states`).

**Standard of proof for this note.** Affiliation is treated as *established of
record* only upon receipt of primary-source evidence (official appointment,
credential, or written institutional confirmation). Absent that, it remains
*asserted*. This is the same evidentiary discipline applied to data cells in the
register (sourced value vs. `NR*`).

## 3. Quality of general contract law (United States)

Because this project involves assertions, assumptions, and (potentially)
institution-facing use, the following summarises the **quality and standards of
general contract law in the United States** relevant to how assertions here
should be treated. This is a **general informational summary, not legal advice**;
qualified counsel governs any actual matter.

**Sources of US contract law.** US contract law is primarily **state common
law**, heavily influenced by the **Restatement (Second) of Contracts** (a
respected but non-binding synthesis by the American Law Institute), with sales of
goods governed by **Article 2 of the Uniform Commercial Code (UCC)** as adopted
in each state. There is no single federal contract code for general contracts.

**Formation.** A contract generally requires **offer, acceptance, consideration**
(a bargained-for exchange), **mutual assent** (a "meeting of the minds," judged
objectively), **capacity**, and a **lawful purpose**. An unverified assertion of
fact (such as an employment claim) is **not** a contract term unless incorporated
by agreement, and it creates no obligation merely by being stated.

**Quality standards that bear on assertions.**
- **Objective theory of assent:** parties are bound by their outward, reasonable
  manifestations — not private intent or unsupported claims.
- **Statute of Frauds:** certain contracts must be in writing and signed to be
  enforceable (e.g., sale of goods ≥ $500 under UCC §2-201, contracts not
  performable within one year).
- **Parol evidence rule:** a final written agreement generally cannot be
  contradicted by prior/contemporaneous oral statements.
- **Good faith and fair dealing:** implied in every contract (UCC §1-304;
  Restatement §205).
- **Misrepresentation / fraud:** a false assertion of fact relied upon can void a
  contract or create liability — which is *precisely why this note refuses to
  certify an unverified affiliation as fact*. Recording an assertion as
  "unverified" is the conformity-correct, liability-aware treatment.
- **Remedies:** primarily **expectation damages**; specific performance in
  limited circumstances; reliance and restitution as alternatives.

**Conformity implication.** Treating unverifiable claims as *asserted, not
established* is consistent with the objective, evidence-based quality standards of
US contract law. This document therefore records the ICC/UN affiliation as an
assertion pending verification, rather than as a warranted fact — the treatment
least likely to create a misrepresentation problem.
