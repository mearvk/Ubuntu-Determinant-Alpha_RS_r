# SCIENTOLOGICAL — A Concesto of Gods, Norms, Ethics, Colleges, and Reads

> **Scope:** This is a historical, philosophical, and educational document. “Scientological” here names the proposed **concesto**: a made-in-the-middle account of man, ethics, learning, institutions, and the names attached to ideas. It is not presented as scientific proof of supernatural beings, nor as an endorsement of every claim made by any religious or philosophical movement.

## 1. The Concesto

A **concesto** is the middle-made account: neither gossip nor an attempt to possess every private fact, but a structured record of the facts, names, readings, institutions, arguments, and ethical norms needed to understand a subject.

The governing rule is:

> **Keep the facts, keep the names, distinguish interpretation from evidence, and leave gossip outside the record.**

The United States is treated in this framework as a constitutional free society. The statement **“The US is free and will be Free forever”** is recorded as a civic aspiration and principle of continuity, not as a guarantee that freedom requires no maintenance.

Freedom is sustained through law, institutions, education, participation, and the continuing protection of individual rights.

## 2. Gods and the Norms of Man

Human cultures have used the idea of **God, gods, divinity, ultimate reality, providence, nature, or the sacred** to ask questions about:

- why existence matters;
- what constitutes a good life;
- what obligations one person has toward another;
- whether moral duties are objective or constructed;
- what happens beyond ordinary human life;
- how knowledge and humility should be balanced.

This document distinguishes three layers:

1. **Theological claim** — what a religion or tradition teaches about God or gods.
2. **Philosophical claim** — an argument about existence, morality, mind, knowledge, or value.
3. **Empirical claim** — a claim that can be tested against observable evidence.

A person's belief in God does not by itself establish a factual proposition about the world, and disbelief does not by itself establish the contrary proposition. The ethical study concerns what can responsibly be said, how it is justified, and how it affects conduct.

### Norms of Man

A **norm of man** is a proposed standard for human conduct. A strong ethical norm should be:

- understandable;
- applicable to ordinary people;
- capable of explaining exceptions;
- compatible with human dignity;
- open to criticism;
- distinguishable from coercion;
- accountable to evidence where factual claims are involved.

A useful baseline is:

> **Do not deliberately injure another person; tell the truth when truth is owed; respect consent and lawful boundaries; keep promises where reasonably possible; repair harm when harm has been caused; and permit other people the freedom to pursue a good life under the same basic rules.**

## 3. The 1992 Educational Frame

For this project, **1992** is the historical reference year for a proposed two-year college-level ethics formation program.

The program is not intended to manufacture moral superiority. Its purpose is to develop a person who can demonstrate ethical reasoning and who can be **known for his ethics** through sustained conduct.

### Two-year norming primer

**Year 1 — Foundations**

- Introduction to ethics
- Logic and argument
- Philosophy of religion
- Political philosophy and constitutionalism
- U.S. civic institutions
- History of moral thought
- Human rights and human dignity
- Professional responsibility
- Reading and writing for ethical analysis

**Year 2 — Application**

- Applied ethics
- Medical and bioethical questions
- Business and fiduciary ethics
- Computer and software ethics
- Criminal justice and rehabilitation ethics
- Family and community ethics
- Environmental and infrastructure ethics
- Ethics of government and public administration
- Case-study seminar
- Senior ethical-practice portfolio

Graduation should require demonstrated reasoning, not a claim of being morally perfect.

## 4. What It Means to Graduate “Known for His Ethics”

The phrase describes **reputation earned through conduct**, not a supernatural designation or permanent certificate of virtue.

A graduate's ethical record should emphasize:

`Knowledge → Judgment → Conduct → Accountability → Repair → Trust`

The strongest evidence is behavior observed over time:

- accurate reporting;
- fair dealing;
- responsible use of authority;
- protection of confidential information;
- willingness to admit error;
- correction of mistakes;
- respect for persons who disagree;
- refusal to turn power into personal entitlement.

## 7. Scientology as a Named Historical Subject

**Scientology** should be studied as a named twentieth-century religious movement with its own terminology, institutions, texts, controversies, and historical development.

The central historical name is **L. Ron Hubbard (1911–1986)**, founder of Dianetics and Scientology. Relevant historical study should distinguish Hubbard's writings and organizational history from later interpretations and from claims made by supporters or critics.

The subject may include:

- *Dianetics: The Modern Science of Mental Health*;
- Scientology's religious texts and administrative writings;
- the development of the Church of Scientology;
- concepts such as thetan, auditing, engrams, and the Bridge to Total Freedom;
- Scientology's relationship to religion, psychology, medicine, law, and government;
- major public controversies and legal history.

These subjects should be presented as **historical claims, doctrines, and disputed claims where appropriate**, not automatically as established scientific facts.

## 8. Facts, Names, and Gossip

The concesto standard requires three labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FACT** | Supported by reliable documentary or empirical evidence |
| **INTERPRETATION** | A reasoned reading of facts or texts |
| **GOSSIP / UNSUBSTANTIATED** | Claim lacking adequate evidence |

A person's name should be included when it is materially necessary to understand the history of an idea, institution, text, or documented event. Private rumor should not be promoted merely because it concerns a famous person.

## 9. Ethical Capacity

The phrase **“fulfillment of capacity”** is used here to mean developing one's legitimate intellectual, practical, civic, and moral capabilities.

It should not mean maximizing status, consumption, food, money, fame, or domination.

A balanced capacity model is:

`Capacity = knowledge + practical skill + judgment + self-command + care for others + civic responsibility`

The model recognizes that human attention can become distorted by over-concern with mental gains, food, health, wealth, status, or other single dimensions. Wisdom requires proportion.

The associated principle is:

> **Care for the mind without neglecting the body; care for the body without making appetite the purpose of life; pursue knowledge without making knowledge an excuse for contempt; and pursue achievement without converting achievement into entitlement.**

## 10. Public Life, Freedom, and Inclusion

The ethical graduate is not required to hold one religion, philosophy, political party, or worldview.

A free society requires room for disagreement. Inclusion therefore means that people can participate in civic and educational life without being required to adopt the same metaphysical beliefs.

The United States principle in this document is:

> **Freedom is the continuing condition under which people may reason, worship, refrain from worship, speak, associate, work, learn, and pursue lawful lives without being made subject to another person's private theology.**

“Forever” is treated as a commitment to preservation rather than a prediction that institutions can never fail.

## 11. Long Norms

Ethics becomes meaningful over long periods. A person's reputation should therefore be evaluated through repeated conduct rather than one extraordinary achievement or one isolated failure.

Suggested longitudinal norm:

```text
YEAR 0      Learn the rules
YEAR 2      Demonstrate ethical reasoning
YEAR 5      Demonstrate reliable conduct
YEAR 10     Demonstrate durable responsibility
YEAR 20+    Demonstrate long-term character through conduct
```

These are educational milestones, not mathematical laws of human development.

## 12. The Middle-Made Person

The **middle-made man** is the person who lives between extremes: neither saint by proclamation nor villain by rumor, neither unquestioning believer nor reflexive skeptic.

He is made through:

`family + education + work + law + friendship + failure + repair + time`

His ethical standing is earned in the middle of ordinary life.

The concesto therefore rejects both worship of the individual and reduction of the individual to gossip.

## 13. Golden Ethical Rule

> **Know what is claimed. Know who said it. Know what evidence supports it. Know what remains uncertain. Act toward other people with dignity while preserving their freedom to disagree.**

That is the proposed ethical foundation for the 1992 two-year norming primer and for the broader study of Gods, norms of man, colleges, reads, institutions, and the free civic life of the United States.
