# United States Governmental Credo — Common Marketplace

## First edition

This credo is a **civic and engineering statement**, not a religious doctrine and not a declaration that government itself is an object of worship. The word "worship" is used only as a metaphor for the highest respect owed to the rule of law, equal civic standing, and a lawful common marketplace.

### The credo

**The United States is a common civic marketplace under law.**

Its government exists by constitutional authority and serves the public; it is not itself sovereign over the conscience of the individual. Public authority must remain accountable to law.

The marketplace is common because persons may participate, exchange, create, contract, associate, compete, and serve under rules that apply through lawful authority rather than private coercion.

The credo therefore holds:

1. **Law before software.** Software serves lawful authority; it does not replace it.
2. **Government serves the public.** A government component is an instrument of public administration, not an object of religious worship.
3. **Equal standing.** A person is not reduced to a transaction, account, identity record, or software descriptor.
4. **Free exchange within law.** Lawful commerce should be capable of operating through predictable, reviewable rules.
5. **Due process and review.** Automated systems must preserve a path for authorized human or institutional review where required.
6. **Evidence is not authority.** Evidence can support a decision; evidence does not manufacture governmental power.
7. **Consent is not payment.** Payment, identity, presence, history, or software classification must never substitute for legally meaningful consent where consent is required.
8. **Public administration is bounded.** Administrative, judicial, legislative, and private commercial functions must not be silently collapsed into one privileged program.
9. **The common marketplace remains plural.** The system accommodates lawful differences among jurisdictions, institutions, persons, and services.
10. **The Constitution and applicable law remain external authority.** Total implements authorized policy; it does not become a government, court, legislature, or sovereign.

## Administrative and jurisdictional divisions

The phrase **"admin / juris"** in this project means a separation of administrative mechanisms from adjudicative authority.

```text
constitutional / statutory authority
              │
              ▼
       lawful administration
              │
              ▼
       reviewed policy
              │
              ▼
            Total
              │
              ▼
        service operation

judicial authority ───────────────► independent review
```

Total may record an administrative policy decision or route an unresolved matter for review. It must not represent its own output as a judgment of a court.

## Supreme Court reference

References in project documentation to **SCOTUS**, the Supreme Court of the United States, or a proposed "SCOTUS 2" concept are descriptive only unless tied to an actual constitutional or statutory institution. This repository does not create a second Supreme Court and does not claim governmental authority.

A software system may model jurisdictional metadata, citations, administrative references, or legal-policy provenance. It cannot thereby become a judicial body.

## 19.2 designation

The project uses **19.2** as a document/version designation where requested. It is not represented as a United States Code section, Supreme Court rule, federal regulation, or other governmental citation unless an actual source is separately identified.

## Marketplace principle

The common marketplace is best represented technically as a set of bounded interfaces:

```text
identity → eligibility → authorization → transaction → service
    │          │             │             │           │
    └──────────┴─────────────┴─────────────┴───────────┘
                         evidence
                            │
                         policy
                            │
                          audit
```

The purpose is predictability and lawful interoperability, not governmental control of every private interaction.

## Final baseline

> **Respect the law. Serve the public. Preserve individual agency. Keep evidence distinct from authority. Keep administration distinct from adjudication. Keep the marketplace open to lawful participation.**

This credo is subordinate to the United States Constitution and applicable federal, state, and local law. It is not legal advice and does not create legal rights or governmental powers.
