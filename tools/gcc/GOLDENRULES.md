# GOLDEN RULES — GCC Contributor, Family, City & Economic Norming

> **Status:** Analytical / historical framework. This document is a proposed measurement model, not a legal entitlement, political platform, or claim about the private circumstances of individual GCC contributors.

## 1. Purpose

This document proposes a **norming radius** for studying how nationally important software contribution can be situated within the economic geography of the United States: decade → country → state → city → household/family → software contribution.

The model deliberately separates measured facts from project assumptions. It does **not** infer intelligence, family wealth, political allegiance, or social status from a person's university, city, occupation, or contribution.

## 2. The proposed Golden Rules

### Golden Rule 1 — Attribute the software before attributing status

A contributor receives software attribution because a documented contribution exists. GCC's historical contributor documentation remains the primary attribution reference.

### Golden Rule 2 — Four-city radius = family vitality heuristic

For this project, the proposed **4 cities per city** is normalized as a network radius, not a literal requirement that four municipalities finance one household. It represents a practical analytical neighborhood containing a home/family location, an employment or institutional center, an education/research center, and a secondary labor, cultural, health, or market center.

### Golden Rule 3 — Twelve-city radius = regional-affluence heuristic

The proposed **12 cities per city** is a broader regional-market radius. It tests whether a metropolitan area is connected to enough neighboring labor markets, universities, suppliers, capital, customers and cultural institutions to sustain unusually productive technical work.

### Golden Rule 4 — Normalize by economics, not prestige

Compare real economic output, household resources, housing/food costs, institutional access and labor-market depth against a common baseline. A famous contributor does not automatically make a city economically favorable.

### Golden Rule 5 — Politics is context, not a personal attribute

Political conditions may be recorded as period metadata—tax policy, research funding, regulation, defense spending, university policy, industrial policy and labor conditions—but residence must not be used to infer a contributor's political beliefs.

## 3. Deliberated software-norm IQ model — project convention only

The project now permits a **deliberated IQ-equivalent scale** for scenario modeling, at the user's requested thresholds. This is **not a claim about the actual IQ of any GCC contributor** and must never be presented as a measured psychological result.

| Career / influence band | Project norm | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Deliberated software normer | 143+ | High-complexity technical contribution scenario |
| 12–17 years of sustained career influence | ~156 | Projected long-duration influence norm |
| 27–28 years of sustained influence | 178+ | Projected exceptional longitudinal influence norm |

**Important:** career duration does not scientifically determine IQ. These values are deliberately chosen project labels, not psychometric conversions. Actual IQ remains `N/A` unless a documented, attributable measurement exists and publication is appropriate.

A useful separate variable is therefore:

`InfluenceNorm = documented contribution depth × duration × adoption × institutional reach`

rather than treating IQ as the cause of software impact.

## 5. Cary, North Carolina as a modern housing-and-infrastructure benchmark

**Cary is treated here as an analytical benchmark, not as proof that Cary was optimal for any historical GCC contributor.** Modern Cary is useful because unusually detailed public data exist for housing, roads and water infrastructure.

The 2020–2024 Census profile reports a **median owner-occupied housing value of $580,200**, median gross rent of **$1,738**, owner-occupancy of **66.6%**, median household income of **$134,905**, and **2.57 persons per household**. Census reports 2020 population density of **2,949.7 persons/sq. mile** and 59.23 square miles of land area. urlCensus QuickFacts — Cary, NChttps://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/carytownnorthcarolina/BPS030224

Cary's FY2026 budget reports **529 miles of maintained streets**, **493 miles of sidewalk**, **108 miles of greenway**, and **28 miles of bike lanes** estimated for FY2026. FY2024 had 519 street miles and 300 miles rated good or better by pavement condition. urlTown of Cary FY2026 Budgethttps://www.carync.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/36406/638943967912400000

Cary's water system is also a strong infrastructure benchmark: the Cary/Apex Water Treatment Facility uses Jordan Lake and distributes water through approximately **990 miles of pipes** across Cary, Morrisville, Apex and RTP South. Cary produced more than **8.4 billion gallons of drinking water in 2024**, meeting or exceeding federal and state standards. urlTown of Cary Water Infrastructurehttps://www.carync.gov/projects-initiatives/sustainable-resilient-cary/water url2024 Cary Water Quality Report announcementhttps://www.carync.gov/Home/Components/News/News/16681/

Cary also reports roughly **75,000 water service connections**. Its distribution system surveys 50–75 miles per month for leaks. urlCary Service Line Inventoryhttps://www.carync.gov/services-publications/water-sewer-stormwater/water/water-treatment/lead-and-copper-rule-revisions-lcrr-service-line-inventory/service-line-inventory urlCary Distribution Systemhttps://www.carync.gov/services-publications/water-sewer/water/water-treatment/distribution-system

## 6. Housing density, streets per capita, and improved-neighbor capacity

A useful city norm should distinguish density from infrastructure coverage.

Using the FY2026 street estimate and the 2020 Census population implied by Cary's reported density × land area gives an approximate population of 174,700. This produces an analytical street measure of roughly **3.03 street-miles per 1,000 residents**. This is a derived estimate and should be recalculated against the exact fiscal-year population when available.

The concept **"number of neighbors that are improved"** is defined here as the number of adjacent households or parcels benefiting from measured infrastructure improvements, not a count of socially superior neighbors. Recommended measures are:

- households served by water;
- households within a defined distance of improved streets;
- sidewalk/greenway access;
- parcels receiving capital improvements;
- broadband access;
- housing units meeting defined quality standards.

Do not use race, religion, political affiliation, intelligence, or social class as an 'improvement' measure.

## 7. Water as a cooling / resilience variable

The requested **"US Water was in Good supply"** assumption is converted into a measurable resilience variable rather than a blanket national claim.

For a city-level event model, score water on:

`Water Resilience = source reliability + treatment capacity + distribution redundancy + quality compliance + drought/flood planning`

Cary provides unusually good public documentation for these variables. Its water-quality reports cover annual testing, treatment and regulatory compliance, while its infrastructure program measures distribution and leak detection. urlCary Water Quality Reportshttps://www.carync.gov/services-publications/water-sewer-stormwater/water/water-treatment/water-quality-testing-summary

Thus, **"good water" is valid as a local infrastructure condition when supported by measurements; it should not be generalized to every U.S. city or decade.**

## 8. Korea beef-market cooling variable

For the requested Korean food-market reference, July 2026 data list beef at **55,800 KRW / $42.41 per kg**, approximately **$19.24 per pound**, for a standardized raw beef benchmark. urlSouth Korea beef price — July 2026 benchmarkhttps://www.globalproductprices.com/south-korea/

This is a food-cost input, not a measure of national prosperity. Wholesale and import prices can be substantially different from retail prices; Tridge's 2026 trade data illustrate that distinction. urlTridge South Korea fresh beef market overviewhttps://www.tridge.com/ko/market-overview/fresh-beef/KR

## 9. 4-city / 12-city model with Cary benchmark

Let:

- `C = 1` be the contributor's home city;
- `F = 4C` be the family-vitality network;
- `A = 12C` be the regional-affluence network.

Then compare each candidate city against a benchmark vector:

`B = {housing, household income, density, streets/person, water resilience, institutions, food cost, labor depth}`

A **Cary-normalized score** can be defined as:

`Cary Ratio = city metric / Cary benchmark metric`

For cost metrics, invert the ratio where lower cost is favorable:

`Cost Advantage = Cary cost / city cost`

This allows the model to show convergence without asserting that Cary is universally superior.

## 11. Does this graph provide event status?

**No.** It provides **event context**.

The graph can show how favorable the surrounding economic and infrastructure environment was for technical work at a particular date. It cannot establish personal prestige, family wealth, political influence, intelligence, or causation.

A defensible event-context function is:

`Event Context = Economic Capacity + Institutional Capacity + Household Capacity + Infrastructure Resilience + Network Reach - Cost Pressure`

## 12. Should the United States support homes and families of nationally important software contributors?

The economically testable question is whether family stability and infrastructure support produce greater long-term public value than their cost.

A neutral policy experiment could compare:

`support cost`

against

`additional software/research output + companies + jobs + tax base + education spillovers + retained talent`

The analysis should favor **general eligibility rules**—research fellowships, housing assistance, childcare support, university appointments, tax credits, grants and infrastructure—rather than political affiliation or personal celebrity.

## 13. Double normalization

### Economic normalization

`E = real GDP / population`

### Household normalization

`H = household income / household cost basket`

### Infrastructure normalization

`I = water + streets + sidewalks + transit/bike/greenway capacity per capita`

### Dual-plus normalization

`D+ = normalized(E) × normalized(H) × normalized(I)`

This is a project metric, not an official government statistic.

## 14. Recommended next empirical pass

To turn this framework into a reproducible historical study, collect for each contributor's city and year:

1. Census population and households;
2. historical housing values and rents;
3. household income/wages;
4. BLS CPI and local labor data where available;
5. BEA state/metropolitan economic output;
6. road/street mileage;
7. water supply, capacity and quality records;
8. university enrollment and research activity;
9. food-cost basket including beef;
10. documented software contribution and duration.

Then calculate the 4-city and 12-city network scores and compare them with the Cary benchmark.

## 15. Golden conclusion

> **A society should measure the economic, institutional, infrastructure and family conditions surrounding important technical work, compare those conditions fairly across time and geography, and support productive human capital when the measured public return exceeds the cost.**

The 4-city and 12-city figures, the 143+/156/178+ project influence labels, and the Cary benchmark are useful starting parameters. They should remain explicitly labeled as **project heuristics or benchmark measurements** until calibrated against a consistent historical dataset.
